How it works

Two sounds, one microphone.

An AI app can pick one microphone input, and generally only one. Your voice reaches it; the voices your Mac is playing do not.

Open Mic mixes the two and hands them over as an ordinary microphone.

Privacy

Your audio stays on your Mac.

Open Mic itself makes no network connections. Audio passes through your Mac's memory on its way to the app you chose, and is neither saved nor sent anywhere else.

macOS asks for Screen Recording permission because capturing system audio falls under it. Open Mic takes only the audio — it never captures or records what is on your screen.

Loopback drivers are well suited to routing audio wherever you like.

Open Mic is not a tool for routing audio freely. It turns your microphone and your Mac's audio into one input. That one thing, with nothing to configure.

Permissions it asks for

Microphone, to capture your voice. Screen Recording, to capture what your Mac is playing.

Open Mic does not record your screen. macOS files system-audio capture under Screen Recording, and there is no narrower permission to ask for.

Audio from the same browser may not come through

Browsers and calling apps have echo cancellation, which stops sound coming out of the speakers from getting back into the microphone.

Because of it, playing a video while transcribing in the same browser can mean that playback is removed from Open Mic's input.

If that happens, keep the side playing the audio and the side using Open Mic in different apps. Play in Safari and transcribe in Chrome, or play in a browser and transcribe in a desktop app.

This is audio processing on the receiving side, so it cannot be turned off from Open Mic's settings.

Telling which half is arriving

The menu bar item is an audio level meter.

  • Left bars: your microphone
  • Right bars: your Mac's audio

When nothing is coming through, it shows which half stopped.

Installing, and removing it completely

Download and open the installer. Your Mac goes quiet for a second or two during the install — not a good moment to be on a call.

To remove everything:

sudo "/Library/Application Support/OpenMic/uninstall.sh"

Removes the app and everything it installed, then checks nothing was left behind.

Reporting a problem

Use GitHub Issues. The app has a Send Feedback item that opens a new issue with the useful details already filled in, for you to read and edit before posting.
