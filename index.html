How it works

Two sounds, one microphone.

Most AI apps take a single microphone input. Your voice reaches it; the voices your Mac is playing do not.

Open Mic mixes your microphone with your Mac's audio and hands the result to the app as an ordinary microphone input.

Privacy

Your audio stays on your Mac.

Open Mic does not record, store, or upload your audio. Audio mixing happens entirely on your Mac.

macOS asks for Screen Recording permission because capturing system audio falls under it. Open Mic takes only the audio — it never captures or records what is on your screen. How the AI app you choose handles the audio is covered by that service's own privacy policy.

Loopback drivers are well suited to routing audio wherever you like.

Open Mic is not a tool for routing audio freely. It turns your microphone and your Mac's audio into one input. That one thing, with nothing to configure.

Permissions it needs

Microphone, to capture your voice. Screen Recording, to capture what your Mac is playing.

Open Mic does not record your screen. macOS files system-audio capture under Screen Recording, and there is no narrower permission to ask for.

When audio from the same browser is hard to pick up

Browsers and calling apps have echo cancellation, which stops sound coming out of the speakers from getting back into the microphone.

Because of it, playing a video while transcribing in the same browser leaves that playback much quieter in Open Mic's input. Sometimes it still works; sometimes it stops being picked up. It depends on the app.

If it stops being picked up, keep the side playing the audio and the side using Open Mic in different browsers or apps. Play in Safari and transcribe in Chrome, or play in a browser and transcribe in a desktop app.

This is audio processing on the receiving side, so it cannot be turned off from Open Mic's settings.

Which half is arriving

The menu bar item is an audio level meter.

  • Left bars: your microphone
  • Right bars: your Mac's audio

When nothing is coming through, it shows which half stopped.

Installing and removing it completely

Download and open the installer. Your Mac goes quiet for a second or two during the install — not a good moment to be on a call.

To remove everything:

sudo "/Library/Application Support/OpenMic/uninstall.sh"

Removes Open Mic and its files, then checks nothing was left behind. The Microphone and Screen Recording entries stay in System Settings; remove those by hand if you want to.

Reporting a problem

Report problems on GitHub Issues. The app's Send Feedback item opens an issue with the useful details already filled in, for you to read and edit before posting.
