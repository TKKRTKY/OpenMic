Privacy

Your audio stays on your Mac.

Open Mic makes no network connections. Audio passes through memory on its way to the app you chose, and that is the end of it.

Loopback drivers are mature, open source and ask for fewer permissions. They are the better choice for general audio routing — Open Mic just does this one thing without the setup.

Permissions it asks for

Microphone, to capture your voice. Screen Recording, to capture what your Mac is playing.

The second one surprises people, so plainly: Open Mic does not record your screen. macOS files system-audio capture under Screen Recording, and there is no narrower permission to ask for.

Telling which half is arriving

The menu bar item is a level meter. The bars on the left are your microphone; the bars on the right are your Mac. When something isn't working it is usually one half, and this shows which — without opening anything.

Installing, and removing it completely

Download and open the installer. Your Mac goes quiet for a second or two during the install — not a good moment to be on a call.

To remove everything:

sudo "/Library/Application Support/OpenMic/uninstall.sh"

Removes the app and everything it installed, then checks nothing was left behind.

For IT and security review

The download includes a document written for reviewers: what is installed where, why a background service is needed, and commands to verify the privacy claims above independently.

Reporting a problem

Use GitHub Issues. The app has a Send Feedback item that opens a new issue with the useful details already filled in, for you to read and edit before posting.
