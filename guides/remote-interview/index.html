Record a remote interview on one audio track

Record your voice and a remote guest’s voice together in QuickTime Player.

Who it’s for

For a remote interview or podcast call where you need one mixed recording rather than separate tracks for editing.

What you need

A Mac running macOS 14.2 or later, Open Mic, QuickTime Player, a calling app, a physical microphone, and headphones. Tell the guest what you will record and obtain any required consent before recording.

Open Mic setup

Set it up

  1. In the call app, keep your physical microphone selected. Listen to the guest through headphones.
  2. Turn on Open Mic. In QuickTime Player, choose File → New Audio Recording, then open the options menu next to the record button and select Open Mic as the microphone.
  3. Record a short sample with both people speaking. Stop and play it back to check both sides, then start a fresh recording for the interview. Stop and save it in QuickTime Player.

Turn Open Mic off when you’re done.

Tips and troubleshooting

If an input is missing, check the Open Mic menu and macOS Privacy & Security settings for microphone and system audio permission (which may be listed under Screen Recording). The left meter shows your mic; the right shows the Mac’s audio.

Open Mic can’t disable echo cancellation in other apps.

Quiet notifications and other playback before you start. Headphones help avoid picking up the guest twice. If the guest sounds quiet, check the call audio and Open Mic’s Mac meter first; also check whether the call app’s echo processing or audio settings have already changed what you hear.

Limits

The file contains a single mixed track; you cannot adjust the guest and host independently afterward. QuickTime Player records and saves it. If you need separate tracks or a guest-side backup, use a recording workflow that provides those.
