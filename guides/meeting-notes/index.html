Capture both sides of a meeting for notes

Send your voice and the people you hear on a call to a separate app for notes or transcription.

Who it’s for

For meeting participants whose transcription app takes one microphone input and would otherwise only hear their own voice.

What you need

A Mac running macOS 14.2 or later, Open Mic, a physical microphone, a meeting app such as Zoom or Google Meet, and a separate notes or transcription app that lets you choose an input. Tell participants and obtain any required consent before sending their audio to that app.

Open Mic setup

Set it up

  1. In the meeting app, keep your physical microphone selected and use your usual audio output. Do not select Open Mic as the meeting microphone.
  2. Turn on Open Mic in the menu bar. In the separate notes or transcription app, select Open Mic as the microphone input.
  3. In a short test call, check that speech from you and a remote participant appears in the transcript. Start and stop transcription in the receiving app.

Turn Open Mic off when you’re done.

Tips and troubleshooting

If an input is missing, check the Open Mic menu and macOS Privacy & Security settings for microphone and system audio permission (which may be listed under Screen Recording). The left meter shows your mic; the right shows the Mac’s audio.

If the receiving app suppresses playback through echo cancellation, try using different apps or browsers for playback and capture. Open Mic cannot turn off that processing. Use headphones to keep speaker playback out of the physical mic.

Mute unrelated apps and notifications before the meeting so they don’t end up in the transcript.

Limits

This is audio into the notes app, not a way to send its voice back into the meeting. Open Mic does not record, transcribe, or label speakers. The receiving app handles the transcript, any speaker labels, and any storage or uploads.
