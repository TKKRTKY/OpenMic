Record a tutorial with narration and app sound

Use QuickTime Player to record your screen with both your narration and the sounds from your demo.

Who it’s for

For people making a walkthrough where an app’s audio matters, such as demonstrating a playback control or explaining a sound cue.

What you need

A Mac running macOS 14.2 or later, Open Mic, QuickTime Player, and a physical microphone. Use headphones to keep speaker playback out of the physical mic.

Open Mic setup

Set it up

  1. Turn on Open Mic in the menu bar.
  2. In QuickTime Player, choose File → New Screen Recording. In the recording controls, choose Options → Microphone → Open Mic, then choose the area to record.
  3. Make a short test: speak and trigger a sound in the app. Stop the recording and play it back to check both sounds. When you’re ready, record and save the tutorial in QuickTime Player.

Turn Open Mic off when you’re done.

Tips and troubleshooting

If an input is missing, check the Open Mic menu and macOS Privacy & Security settings for microphone and system audio permission (which may be listed under Screen Recording). The left meter shows your mic; the right shows the Mac’s audio.

Open Mic can’t disable echo cancellation in other apps.

Silence notifications and unrelated apps before recording: choosing a screen area doesn’t restrict which Mac audio Open Mic captures. If the demo depends on a calling app, check whether that app’s echo cancellation changes what you hear before you record.

Limits

QuickTime Player records the screen and saves the file; Open Mic supplies only one mixed, mono audio input. Narration and app sound aren’t saved as separate tracks. Grant any screen capture permission macOS requests for the recording separately from Open Mic’s system audio permission.
